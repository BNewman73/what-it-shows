The problems

parser: the shared parser reads dates month first. Every tenant on file writes the day first. A booking on 7 April goes out on 4 July. This is the fix #4471 already has queued. Ticket.

hint: the booking-v3 form tells people to type month first. booking-v3 prints MM/DD/YYYY under its date field, then reads the date in the tenant’s order, day first. People type what the hint says, so the date is swapped before the parser sees it. Only reports that came in through that form, from tenants on booking-v3. The parser fix does not touch it. Ticket.

one-day: bookings move by one day. “Client in Lisbon got Tuesday. Client in Toronto got Monday. Same meeting.” No day and month are swapped here, so neither fix above closes these. It looks like a time-zone or day-boundary problem. The desk had already read these reports as a one-day move (desk-notes.json), but #4471’s table counts them under parser because of their tenant. Ticket.

How the reports were placed

  • One report per id. The ticket export comes in overlapping pages, so some reports appear twice.
  • By tenant, not by intake tag. Intake guesses a tag from the reporter’s browser. The tenant’s locale and booking form decide the cause (src/lib/kb/causes.ts). The two disagree often; the table shows how often.
  • One-day moves first. A report the desk read as a one-day move is one-day, whatever its tenant (src/lib/kb/problems.ts).
  • Every body was read. All other reports describe a day and month swapped, or say only that the date was wrong.

Lessons

  • A big ticket can be several bugs with the same symptom. Count by cause, not by subject.
  • A footnote under a table is a finding nobody owns. Give it a ticket.
