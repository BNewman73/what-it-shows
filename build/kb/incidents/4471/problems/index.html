Counted the desk’s way: one report per id, under the cause its tenant gives it. The one-day moves stay counted under parser, where #4471’s own table files them, and are counted again as their own problem.

The problems

parser: the shared parser read dates month first. Every tenant on file writes the day first. A booking on 7 April went out on 4 July. Fixed (fix(dates): parse dd/mm for eu locales), which is what closed #4471. Ticket.

hint: the booking-v3 form told people to type month first. booking-v3 prints MM/DD/YYYY under its date field, then reads the date in the tenant’s order, day first. People typed what the hint said, so the date was swapped before the parser saw it. Only reports that came in through that form, from tenants on booking-v3. Won’t fix: booking-v3 is retired. Tenants still on it need to move off it. Ticket.

one-day: bookings move by one day. “Client in Lisbon got Tuesday. Client in Toronto got Monday. Same meeting.” No day and month are swapped here, so neither the parser fix nor retiring the form closes these. It looks like a time-zone or day-boundary problem. The desk had already read these reports as a one-day move (desk-notes.json), but #4471’s table only footnotes them under parser. Open. Ticket.

How the reports were placed

  • One report per id. The ticket export comes in overlapping pages, so some reports appear twice.
  • By tenant, not by intake tag. Intake guesses a tag from the reporter’s browser. The tenant’s locale and booking form decide the cause (src/lib/kb/causes.ts). The two disagree often; the table shows how often.
  • One-day moves from the desk’s notes (src/lib/kb/problems.ts).
  • Every body was read. All other reports describe a day and month swapped, or say only that the date was wrong.

Lessons

  • A big ticket can be several bugs with the same symptom. Count by cause, not by subject.
  • A footnote under a table is a finding nobody owns. Give it a ticket, or it closes with the parent.
